PART 1 | UNDERSTANDING THE ACT
1. What problem did this Act solve?
Before the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006, food regulation was spread across multiple laws and
orders. The Act's central architecture is to consolidate food laws, establish FSSAI, prescribe
science-based standards and regulate food manufacture, storage, distribution, sale and import.
MEMORY HOOK
Many food laws → one integrated framework → FSSAI + State enforcement → standards → safe &
wholesome food.

PART 2 | CHAPTER I — PRELIMINARY &
DEFINITIONS
Sections 1–3
Section 1: Short title, extent and commencement. The Act is the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006; it
extends to the whole of India and provisions commence by notification.
Section 2: Parliament declares it expedient in the public interest that the Union should take control of the
food industry.
Section 3 — Definitions
Definitions are a major FSO examination area because small statutory distinctions can change the
answer.
Term Exam-ready meaning
Adulterant Material which is or could be employed to make food unsafe, sub-standard, misbranded
or containing extraneous matter.

Term Exam-ready meaning
Consumer Persons and families purchasing and receiving food to meet their personal needs.
Food Broad statutory category including processed/unprocessed food, infant food, packaged
drinking water, alcoholic drink, chewing gum and specified water used in
manufacture/preparation/treatment.
Food Business Any undertaking, profit or non-profit, public or private, involved in manufacture,
processing, packaging, storage, transportation, distribution, import or sale of food.
Food Business Operator Person by whom food business is carried on/owned and who is responsible for

Food Safety Assurance that food is acceptable for human consumption according to its intended use.

Hazard Biological, chemical or physical agent in food, or condition of food, with potential to
cause an adverse health effect.
Misbranded Food Food whose identity, description, presentation, labelling or claims are false, misleading
or deceptive in the statutory circumstances.
Sub-standard Food Food that does not meet specified standards but does not render the food unsafe.
Unsafe Food Food whose nature, substance or quality is affected so as to render it injurious to health.
Sale Broadly includes cash, credit, exchange, wholesale/retail, offer/exposure/possession for
sale and attempt to sell.
HPSC / FSO EXAM ALERT
Know the difference: sub-standard ≠ unsafe; misbranded = identity/representation problem; unsafe
= health problem.

PART 3 | CHAPTER II — FSSAI
Sections 4–17
Section 4 — Establishment of FSSAI
The Central Government establishes the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India. It is a body
corporate with perpetual succession and a common seal, capable of holding property, contracting and
suing/being sued. Headquarters: Delhi.
Section 5 — Composition
Component Number / point
Chairperson 1
Members 22
Women At least one-third of the 22 members
Government representatives 7
Industry 2
Consumer organisations 2
Food technologists/scientists 3
States/UTs by zones 5
Farmers' organisations 2
Retailers' organisations 1
MEMORY HOOK
22 members + Chairperson. Chairperson age ceiling = 65; other member = 62.
Sections 6–8 — Selection, term and removal
• Selection Committee is chaired by the Cabinet Secretary.
• Chairperson/members other than ex-officio members: 3-year term; eligible for another 3 years.
• Chairperson maximum age: 65 years; member maximum age: 62 years.
• Removal grounds include insolvency, specified conviction, incapacity, prejudicial financial/other
interest and abuse of position against public interest.
Sections 9–10 — Officers and CEO
The Chief Executive Officer is not below the rank of Additional Secretary to the Government of India,
serves as Member-Secretary and handles day-to-day administration, programmes, implementation,
budget and administrative control.
Sections 11–15 — Advisory and scientific machinery
The Central Advisory Committee brings together industry, agriculture, consumers, research bodies,
laboratories, Commissioners of Food Safety and the Chairperson of the Scientific Committee. It must

Scientific Panels provide specialist advice; the Scientific Committee coordinates scientific opinion and

Section 16 — Functions of FSSAI
FSSAI regulates and monitors food manufacture, processing, distribution, sale and import to ensure safe
and wholesome food. Its functions include standards, additive/contaminant/pesticide-residue limits,
imported-food controls, laboratory recognition, sampling and analysis, risk assessment, scientific advice,
emerging-risk surveillance, crisis management, consumer information, training and international/Codex
work.
Section 18 — General principles preview
Food regulation is science-based, risk-based, proportionate, transparent and protective of human health
and consumer interests.
PART 4 | CHAPTER III — GENERAL PRINCIPLES
Section 18 — Core principles
• Protect human life and health and consumer interests, while considering fair trade practices.
• Base risk management on risk assessment and relevant factors.
• Apply a precautionary approach where possible harmful effects are identified but scientific uncertainty
remains.
• Measures should be proportionate and not more trade-restrictive than necessary.
• Provisional measures should be reviewed within a reasonable period.
• Public should be informed when reasonable grounds indicate that food may pose a risk.
• If one food in a batch/lot/consignment fails safety requirements, the whole batch/lot/consignment is
presumed non-compliant until contrary evidence.
• Scientific risk assessment must be independent, objective and transparent.
MEMORY HOOK
Risk assessment = 4 steps. Risk analysis = 3 components.
HPSC / FSO EXAM ALERT
Urgency involving food safety/public health can justify regulation without ordinary consultation;
such emergency regulation is limited to the statutory period.

Section 23 = labelling/packaging. Section 24 = advertisement/unfair trade practice. Do not

Section 25 — Imports
Food imports must comply with the Act and cannot include unsafe, misbranded, sub-standard or
prohibited/non-compliant food, or food requiring a licence where the required licence is absent.
PART 6 | CHAPTER VI — FBO RESPONSIBILITIES
Sections 26–28
Section 26 — Responsibilities of FBO
Every FBO must ensure that food satisfies the Act, rules and regulations at all stages under its control. An
FBO must not manufacture, store, sell or distribute prohibited or non-compliant food and must comply
with prescribed licensing/registration requirements.
Section 27 — Liability
Operator Typical liability focus
Manufacturer / packer Liability for food failing statutory requirements in the circumstances specified.
Wholesaler / distributor Includes expiry, unsafe/misbranded food, improper storage, unidentifiable
source and knowingly receiving unsafe food.
Seller Includes expired food, unhygienic handling, misbranding, unidentifiable source
and knowingly receiving unsafe food.

PART 7 | CHAPTER VII — ENFORCEMENT
Sections 29–42
Section 30 — Commissioner of Food Safety
The Commissioner is appointed by the State Government and oversees state implementation. Functions
include public-health prohibitions, surveys, training, uniform implementation and prosecution sanction
for offences punishable with imprisonment.
Section 31 — Licence & registration
Food business
↓
Ordinary FBO → Licence
↓
Petty FBO → Registration
No person may commence/carry on food business except under the required licence, subject to the
registration route for petty food businesses. If a licence application is neither granted nor rejected within
two months, the statutory consequences in Section 31 apply.
Section 32 — Improvement Notice
Issued by the Designated Officer when an FBO has failed to comply. It specifies the grounds, failure,
corrective measures and a reasonable compliance period. The period cannot be less than 14 days.
Non-compliance can lead to suspension and, after due process, cancellation.
Sections 33–34 — Prohibition
Instrument Core idea
Section 33 — Prohibition Order Court-based prohibition following conviction where health risk exists.
Section 34 — Emergency Urgent action where a health-risk condition requires emergency intervention.
Prohibition
Section 36 — Designated Officer
Appointed by the Commissioner of Food Safety; not below the rank of Sub-Divisional Officer. There is a
Designated Officer for each district. Core functions: licensing, prohibition of non-compliant food, receiving
samples/reports, prosecution-related action, records and complaint investigation.
Section 37 — Food Safety Officer
FOOD SAFETY & STANDARDS ACT, 2006 | HPSC FSO Page 8

The Commissioner of Food Safety appoints qualified Food Safety Officers by notification for assigned local
areas.
Section 38 — Powers of FSO
• Take samples of food/substances intended for sale or found on relevant premises where evidence may
be required.
• Seize food appearing to contravene the Act/regulations.
• Enter and inspect premises where food/adulterant is manufactured, stored, exhibited or sold.
• Pay the usual public-sale rate for a sample taken.
• Destroy perishable food after written notice where it is deteriorated and unfit for human consumption.
• Exercise additional seizure/document/witness powers in accordance with the statutory procedure.
Section 39 — Liability of FSO
Vexatious or unreasonable seizure, or other unjustified injurious acts in exercise of powers, can attract a
penalty up to ₹1 lakh. A false complaint against an FSO, if proved false, attracts a fine of ₹50,000 to ₹1
lakh.
Section 40 — Purchaser may have food analysed
A purchaser other than an FSO may have food analysed on payment of the prescribed fee, after
informing the FBO at purchase. If the report establishes non-compliance, the purchaser is entitled to
refund of the analysis fee.
Section 41 — Search/seizure
An FSO may search and seize where there is reasonable doubt of involvement in a food offence, and
must inform the Designated Officer. Relevant CrPC provisions apply as far as the Act permits.

PART 9 | CHAPTER IX — OFFENCES & PENALTIES
Sections 48–67
Section 48 — General provisions relating to offences
Food may be rendered injurious by adding a substance, using an ingredient, abstracting constituents or
subjecting food to a process/treatment with knowledge of sale/distribution. Safety assessment considers
normal use, handling, consumer information, cumulative toxic effects and vulnerable consumers.

PART 10 | CHAPTER X — ADJUDICATION, TRIBUNAL
& COURTS
Sections 68–80
Section 68 — Adjudication
State Government notifies an officer not below the rank of Additional District Magistrate as Adjudicating
Officer. The person must receive a reasonable opportunity of representation. The officer has powers of a
civil court for adjudication.
Section 69 — Compounding
The Commissioner may empower the Designated Officer to accept composition money from specified
petty food operators. Maximum composition amount: ₹1 lakh. Offences punishable with imprisonment
cannot be compounded.
Sections 70–71 — Appellate Tribunal
The Food Safety Appellate Tribunal hears appeals from Adjudicating Officer decisions. Its Presiding
Officer must be or have been a District Judge. The Tribunal is guided by principles of natural justice and
has specified civil-court-like powers.
Sections 72–80 — Courts and defences
Section Core point
72 Civil court jurisdiction barred for matters entrusted to Adjudicating Officer/Tribunal; injunction
restrictions apply.
73 Summary trial for offences not triable by Special Court; maximum imprisonment in summary
conviction = 1 year.
74 Special Courts for specified serious offences; Public Prosecutor requires at least 7 years'
advocacy/relevant legal experience.
76 Appeal from Special Court to High Court within 45 days; statutory bench requirement applies.
77 Ordinary cognizance limitation = 1 year; Commissioner may approve extended prosecution up to 3
years for recorded reasons.
78 Court may implead importer/manufacturer/distributor/dealer when evidence warrants.
79 Magistrate/court punishment powers subject to statutory limits.
80 Defences include due diligence/reasonable precautions in specified circumstances; mere mistaken
reasonable belief is not a defence.
HPSC / FSO EXAM ALERT
High-yield appeal numbers: Tribunal → High Court = 60 days; Special Court → High Court = 45 days.
Ordinary prosecution limitation = 1 year; extension = up to 3 years.

High-probability HPSC questions
Q1 FSSAI was established under which section?
Answer: Section 4.
Q2 Who appoints the Food Safety Officer?
Answer: Commissioner of Food Safety — Section 37.
Q3 Who is the Designated Officer and what is the minimum rank?
Answer: District-level officer appointed by Commissioner; not below Sub-Divisional Officer — Section 36.
Q4 How many members are in the Food Authority?
Answer: 22 members, plus the Chairperson.
Q5 What is the FSO's core statutory power section?
Answer: Section 38.
Q6 Into how many parts is a normal sample divided under Section 47?
Answer: Four parts.
Q7 What happens when analytical reports are at variance?
Answer: Referral Food Laboratory analysis; its decision is final.
Q8 Maximum penalty for misleading advertisement?
Answer: Up to ₹10 lakh.
Q9 Maximum penalty for misbranded food?
Answer: Up to ₹3 lakh.
Q10 What is the punishment where unsafe food causes death?
Answer: 7 years to life imprisonment and minimum ₹10 lakh fine.
Q11 Who adjudicates monetary penalties?
Answer: Adjudicating Officer — not below Additional District Magistrate.
Q12 Can an imprisonment offence be compounded?
Answer: No.
Q13 Who makes Central rules?
Answer: Central Government — Section 91.
Q14 Who makes FSSAI regulations?
Answer: Food Authority, with previous Central Government approval — Section 92.
Q15 What is the overriding provision?
Answer: Section 89.
